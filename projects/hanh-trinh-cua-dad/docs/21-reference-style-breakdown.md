## Useful principles extracted

### 1. Build a still before building motion

The strongest paper animation workflow begins with a designed composition:
- paper material;
- cut edge;
- subject placement;
- shadow;
- hierarchy.

Only then define movement.

This project therefore requires one hero still per scene before animation.

### 2. The stop-motion feeling comes from discrete physical changes

Paper should:
- jump a few pixels;
- rotate a fraction;
- settle;
- hold;
- move again.

The goal is not "bad low-FPS animation".
The goal is the feeling of a human moving physical pieces between photographs.

### 3. Paper texture must persist across scenes

Lock:
- background paper family;
- yellow highlight family;
- photo-card edge;
- shadow direction;
- grain level;
- cutout edge language.

Do not regenerate each scene's paper style independently.

### 4. Scene motion is cause-and-effect

A piece should move because:
- narration introduces it;
- another card pushes it;
- timeline advances;
- map route reaches it;
- the previous scene physically hands off to it.

This prevents generic floating elements.

### 5. Trajectory should be reviewed before motion

For important moving pieces:
- show numbered positions;
- review the path;
- then animate.

For Dad project:
- Australia→Vietnam route;
- Dad/kangaroo card entry;
- buffalo boss entry;
- 1996 route;
- 8-student card build;
- 2006 handoff;
- archive collapse into final Dad card.

### 6. Paper stop-motion and editorial documentary are not identical

The film uses paper motion as a visual language, but factual evidence remains legible.

Use more tactile paper in:
- comedy;
- map transitions;
- year changes;
- graphic explanation.

Use less tactile distortion in:
- Dad portrait;
- student archive;
- graduation photos;
- evidence/history.

## Stop-motion cadence by act

### 0:00–0:24 — Comedy

Paper cadence:
strong.

Use on-twos plus occasional on-threes.

Visible hand-placed feel:
yes.

### 0:24–1:20 — Factual origin

Paper cadence:
moderate.

Maps/routes/cards can step.
Dad/evidence stays calm.

### 1:20–1:51 — Program growth

Paper cadence:
moderate-fast.

Timeline and archive cards assemble.

### 1:51–2:00 — Emotional ending

Paper cadence:
minimal.

Archive settles.
Dad face stable.
Final motto clean.

## Failure modes

Reject scenes that look like:
- PowerPoint;
- Canva template;
- generic motion-graphics preset;
- AI B-roll unrelated to narration;
- perfectly smooth vector animation;
- chaotic scrapbook with no hierarchy;
- nonstop jitter;
- multiple competing yellow highlights;
- fake archive.

## Key production distinction

**Remotion determines every frame.**

AI can create:
- one starting still;
- a cutout asset;
- an optional moving animal clip.

AI does not determine:
- full scene layout;
- factual text;
- timing;
- year labels;
- transition logic;
- final edit.
