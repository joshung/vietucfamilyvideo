## Verified first-party facts from supplied website content

### Adelaide — the idea begins

While living and working in **Adelaide, South Australia**, Dad Stevenson discussed with **Sơ Nien**, a lecturer at the University of Adelaide, his wish to help disadvantaged Vietnamese students who had ability, determination and academic potential but lacked sufficient financial resources to continue university or college study in Vietnam.

### Early network in Vietnam

Through Catholic church relationships, Sơ Nien asked **Cha Nguyễn Văn Tuyến**, serving at a church in Tân Bình District, Ho Chi Minh City, to help identify disadvantaged students who were studying at universities and colleges in Ho Chi Minh City.

### 1996 — Dad meets Đoàn Minh Nam

In **1996**, Đoàn Minh Nam met Dad Stevenson during Dad's trip through:
- Đông Bắc;
- Cao Bằng;
- Bắc Cạn;
- Lạng Sơn;
- Hạ Long.

At the time, anh Nam was Dad's tour guide.

### 1997 — Dad returns and the scholarship plan becomes concrete

In **1997**, Dad returned to Vietnam.

Anh Nam began organizing services for Dad in Vietnam such as hotel bookings, meals and work-trip logistics.

At a meeting at the **SunWay Hotel, Phạm Đình Hổ Street, Hai Bà Trưng District, Hà Nội**, Dad discussed the scholarship program with anh Nam:
- target students;
- possible scholarship level;
- selection criteria;
- diligence;
- academic performance;
- conduct and character.

### First 8 students

The first group Dad supported consisted of **8 students**.

The website says many were studying in the Social Protection faculty at the Open University in Ho Chi Minh City, with others studying finance/accounting at Hồng Bàng University.

The first meeting took place at the church where Cha Nguyễn Văn Tuyến worked.

Anh Nam interpreted and recorded information about:
- academic situation;
- family circumstances;
- hometown;
- student plans.

### First program name

The scholarship program first operated as:

**Stevenson Scholarship Programme**

with anh Nam collaborating in Vietnam.

### 1997–2006 — growth

From **1997 to 2006**, the number of supported students increased each year.

Students were introduced through friends, former/current scholarship members, community relationships, and later through wider contacts including the Vietnam Student Association and Vietnam National University, Hanoi.

### 2006 — handover and renaming

In **2006**, Đoàn Minh Nam transferred the work to **Nguyễn Hoàng Cung**.

In the same year, the program changed its name to the present:

**Viet Uc Family Scholarship Program**

## Program purpose and principles

The supplied website says the program aims to help Vietnamese students in especially difficult financial circumstances who have clear future goals.

Support can include:
- tuition;
- accommodation;
- books;
- travel;
- medical needs;
- family crises.

Selection considers:
- financial need;
- academic effort/performance;
- attitude;
- interviews;
- family visits.

Important principles:
- support can continue through the course depending on study effort and circumstances;
- support is **unconditional**;
- there is **no repayment obligation**;
- there is no discrimination by religion, ethnicity, field of study, gender or relationship;
- graduates are encouraged, if able, to help others facing hardship.
